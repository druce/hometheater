# Dirac Live (Room Correction, Bass Control, ART)

**For a one-sub 5.1 room the useful Dirac purchase is Room Correction plus Bass Control, which costs $299 on the Arcam AVA15 and Onkyo TX-RZ50 (Room Correction included) and $558 to $599 on the Denon X3800H/X3900H and Marantz CINEMA 50 models (dirac.com, 2026-11-08), while the Denon X2900H and Marantz CINEMA 70s Series 2 cannot take Bass Control at all.**

Used by: [Room correction & bass management](../criteria/room-correction.md) · [Value, reliability & resale](../criteria/value-and-reliability.md).

## What it does

Dirac Live is sold as three licences that stack: Room Correction, Bass Control, and Active Room Treatment (ART). Both add-ons require Room Correction, and ART also requires Bass Control whenever one or more subwoofers are connected (Dirac device pages, 2026-11-08). Room Correction measures each speaker from several positions and builds filters that adjust both frequency response and timing, against built-in or hand-drawn target curves; the claimed benefits (larger sweet spot, better imaging) are manufacturer claims (Dirac Room Correction page, 2026-11-08). Calibration runs from Dirac's Windows/Mac software or a phone app, and the filters then run on the receiver. What any correction can and cannot do against small-room modes is covered in [room modes and correction limits](../concepts/room-modes-and-correction-limits.md).

Receivers carrying it: [Denon AVR-X3900H](../products/denon-avr-x3900h.md), [Denon AVR-X3800H](../products/denon-avr-x3800h.md), [Denon AVR-X2900H](../products/denon-avr-x2900h.md), [Marantz CINEMA 70s Series 2](../products/marantz-cinema-70s-series-2.md), [Marantz CINEMA 50 Series 2](../products/marantz-cinema-50-series-2.md), [Marantz CINEMA 50](../products/marantz-cinema-50.md), [Onkyo TX-RZ50](../products/onkyo-tx-rz50.md), [NAD T 758 V3i](../products/nad-t-758-v3i.md), [Arcam AVA15](../products/arcam-ava15.md).

### Bass Control

Bass Control works with a single subwoofer. Dirac's Bass Control page says it time-aligns and levels the sub against the speakers with one sub or several (Dirac Bass Control page, expanded capture, 2026-11-08). The Arcam manual gives the practical difference: with Room Correction alone, crossovers and sub distance and level are set by hand on the receiver, while Bass Control computes them (Arcam AVA15/AVA25 owner's manual, p.40, 2026-08). For one sub, that automation and phase alignment across the crossover apply; multi-sub optimisation does not. **No source quantifies the single-sub gain**, and no independent measurement of it was found.

Arcam's manual also lists a Bass Management filter type, Room Correction plus crossover settings, without saying which licence enables it (p.40).

### Active Room Treatment (ART)

ART drives all speakers to generate cancellation signals against room resonances in real time, and Dirac describes it as its most advanced tier (Dirac ART page, 2026-11-08). It runs only on the device itself, and the Arcam manual requires nine measurement positions for it (p.40). With a sub, ART means buying all three licences, and its effect is a manufacturer claim with no independent measurement.

## Versions and tiers

Room Correction comes in two bandwidths: Limited corrects 20–500 Hz and Full corrects 20–20,000 Hz. On the Denon X3900H page Limited is $259, Full is $349, and the Limited-to-Full upgrade is $99, so starting Limited and upgrading later costs $358, $9 more than buying Full outright (Dirac X3900H page, 2026-11-08). Bass Control and ART are $299 each wherever offered. Bundle prices are the same on every full-stack Denon and Marantz page, against struck-through list prices that may mark a temporary discount. The $899 top of Dirac's advertised bundle range matches no device page read.

## Measured and observed results

The only independent measurement in the evidence is Ethan Winer's test of PC Dirac Live in a 33 × 18 ft studio, which is neither an AV receiver nor Bass Control (Winer, undated, update 2020-09). A nine-position calibration flattened the response at the main seat, less so nearby, and moved decay times both ways: ringing at 49 Hz fell from about 290 to 240 ms, while decay at 37 Hz rose from about 375 to 450 ms, and a high-Q boost filling a 57 Hz null added ringing of its own (measured, smoothing off). A single-position calibration flattened that spot further but degraded within a foot or two. Winer, who sells acoustic treatment, argues for cutting peaks and limiting boosts to about 5 dB, which Dirac lets the user do.

Onkyo's RZ50 firmware log documents implementation bugs: the Bass Control crossover was applied incorrectly from the 28 Oct 2024 firmware until a 1 Jul 2025 fix that required re-exporting filters, some speaker configurations got wrong Dirac filters until 12 Mar 2025, and a May 2022 revision of the bundled mic's curve required re-measurement (Onkyo RZ50 firmware notes, 2026-03). ASR's RZ50 review found the phone-app route with the receiver's own mic workable but did not test the correction result (ASR, 2022-02).

## Limits

- **Pure Direct and Direct bypass Dirac** on the Denon X3900H, so corrected music needs a mode such as Stereo (X3900H manual p.133).
- Changing the speaker layout after calibration disables Dirac until it is re-run (X3900H manual p.275).
- On Denon and Marantz, Audyssey and Dirac coexist in two speaker presets, with Dirac results saved to Preset 2 on first export (X3900H manual p.128); see [Audyssey MultEQ](audyssey-multeq.md).
- On the Arcam, distances, levels and crossovers calculated by Bass Control or ART cannot be adjusted on the receiver after upload (Arcam manual p.40), so every change means a new filter.
- Whether a Dirac microphone comes with a Denon or Marantz upgrade is unknown; their manuals list only the Audyssey mic, and Dirac's RC page names a UMIK-1-class external mic. Onkyo, NAD and Arcam supply one.
- Unknown: which tier gates custom target curves and third-party microphones (NAD's data sheet ties both to the full version; Arcam's manual and Dirac's RC page do not tier them), and whether the $299 Bass Control licence covers any number of subs. Onkyo's 2024 announcement called it a single version; Dirac's pages show no such split now.
