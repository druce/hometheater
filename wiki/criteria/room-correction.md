# Criterion 1: Room correction & bass management

**Dirac Room Correction plus Bass Control is the best one-sub setup on offer, and the Denon AVR-X3900H (XT32 included, $599 to add both) and Onkyo TX-RZ50 (Room Correction included, $299 to add Bass Control) reach it best.**

## Why this matters here

In a small closed room with one subwoofer, the room's modes decide most of what you hear in the bass (Welti and Devantier, J. AES 2006), and that is the region every system here works on hardest. Two limits from [room modes and the limits of correction](../concepts/room-modes-and-correction-limits.md) shape what to expect: correction flattens the seat it was calibrated for, and Winer's measured Dirac result faded within a foot or two and moved some decay times the wrong way. The job in this room is therefore narrow. Get the single sub's crossover, distance, level and phase right against the mains at the main seat, then apply moderate EQ. Dirac Bass Control automates that, Audyssey and ARC partly automate it, and the mechanics are on [bass management and crossovers](../concepts/bass-management-and-crossovers.md).

The second consequence is cost. Three candidates include Dirac Room Correction, six sell it, and two have no Dirac path at all, so the brand label says less than the total price of the licence you actually want. With an external streamer settled and the budget open ([my system](../start-here/my-system.md)), the ranking weighs capability first and the all-in price second. One practical point applies to every Denon and Marantz: Pure Direct and Direct switch correction off, so corrected music needs Stereo or another processed mode (X3900H manual p.133).

## What good looks like

| Level | Threshold for this room | Cheapest candidate that reaches it (2026-11-08) |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum | Included multipoint auto-calibration that sets sub distance, level and crossover, stored and switchable | Denon AVR-X2900H, $1,349, MultEQ XT included |
| Good | Dirac Room Correction (at least Limited, 20–500 Hz) or XT32 with an editable target, included or for a modest licence | Onkyo TX-RZ50, $1,399.99, full-bandwidth Dirac Room Correction included |
| Best | Dirac Room Correction plus Bass Control, so the sub's crossover, distance, level and phase alignment are computed from the measurement | Onkyo TX-RZ50, $1,698.99 with the $299 Bass Control licence |

ART is left out of the table: with a sub it needs all three licences, and its benefit is a Dirac claim with no independent measurement ([Dirac Live](../systems/dirac-live.md)).

**Sony DCAC IX (no candidate carries it).** Sony's Digital Cinema Auto Calibration IX shows what an included system without Dirac or Audyssey does. On the STR-AN1000 ($1,149.99) and ES models such as the STR-AZ3000ES ($2,199.99; Sony US, 2026-11-08) it sets level, distance, speaker size (the crossover), EQ and phase, from two measurements at a single position with the microphone rotated 90 degrees between them rather than a multi-seat sweep (STR-AN1000 help guide, pp.94, 96). The target is one of three presets, Full Flat, Engineer (Sony's unspecified room standard) or Front Reference, with no editable curve (p.194). Pure Direct switches it off, phase matching may run the unit below the input's sample rate, and there is no paid tier (help guide; AZ3000ES product page). No independent measurement of it is in the evidence. It gives less control over the bass than the eight-position MultEQ XT on the $1,349 X2900H, which costs $199 more than the AN1000.

## How the candidates rank

### Best for this criterion

- [Denon AVR-X3900H](../products/denon-avr-x3900h.md): XT32 in the box plus every Dirac licence, with Audyssey and Dirac in separate presets for a same-room comparison; Room Correction plus Bass Control is $599 as a bundle ($558 with Limited), $2,448 all in.
- [Onkyo TX-RZ50](../products/onkyo-tx-rz50.md): full-bandwidth Dirac and a microphone included and Bass Control $299, $1,698.99 for the pair, $300 more than the RZ30 for the same licences; no ART, no Audyssey fallback, and Bass Control applied the crossover wrongly until the 2025-07-01 firmware.
- [Onkyo TX-RZ30](../products/onkyo-tx-rz30.md): the RZ50's arrangement, full-bandwidth Dirac and a mic included and Bass Control $299 (dirac.com, 2026-11-09), $1,398.99 for the pair and the cheapest route to this level; two sub outputs with independent distance and level (manual pp.111–112); no ART, no Audyssey fallback.
- [Arcam AVA15](../products/arcam-ava15.md): Room Correction and a microphone included (Full bandwidth inferred), Bass Control $299 and ART available, but $4,798.95 all in, and Bass Control's computed values cannot be edited on the receiver.
- [Marantz CINEMA 50 Series 2](../products/marantz-cinema-50-series-2.md): the X3900H's stack, XT32 plus the $599 Room Correction and Bass Control bundle, at $3,599 all in; whether a Limited tier is sold is not shown.
- [Denon AVR-X3800H](../products/denon-avr-x3800h.md): the same Dirac licences for $1,998 all in and the only Denon on the MultEQ-X list, but its built-in Audyssey tier is unconfirmed.
- [Marantz CINEMA 50](../products/marantz-cinema-50.md): the full Dirac stack for $3,098 all in and MultEQ-X listed, with XT32 resting on a single compare tile and no captured manual.

### Good

- [Anthem MRX 540 8K](../products/anthem-mrx-540-8k.md): ARC Genesis included with target-curve and crossover control and nothing to buy, but distances are entered by hand, automatic sub phase alignment is documented for Anthem's STR line only, and ARC has no independent test.
- [Denon AVR-X2900H](../products/denon-avr-x2900h.md): MultEQ XT included and Dirac Room Correction for $259–$349, but Bass Control is not supported, so the sub is set by hand under Dirac.
- [NAD T 758 V3i](../products/nad-t-758-v3i.md): Dirac Room Correction included, most likely Limited with a $99 Full upgrade (inferred from the T 758 V3), and no Bass Control.
- [Marantz CINEMA 70s Series 2](../products/marantz-cinema-70s-series-2.md): the X2900H's arrangement, XT plus paid Dirac Room Correction without Bass Control, at $1,500 before the licence.

### Weaker

- [Yamaha RX-A4A](../products/yamaha-rx-a4a.md): YPAO's Low Frequency mode detects problem frequencies and leaves Q and gain to be set by hand, and there is no Dirac or other paid upgrade.

## Open questions

Reader-facing list: [open questions](../reference/open-questions.md). These would move the ranking:

- **Is a Dirac microphone supplied with Denon and Marantz upgrades** (open question #19)? If not, a UMIK-1-class mic adds to the X3900H, X3800H and CINEMA 50 totals and widens the RZ50's cost lead.
- **The Arcam's included bandwidth and the NAD V3i's tier** (#17). A Limited-only Arcam would drop below the Marantz models; a Full V3i would lift the NAD above the X2900H.
- **Limited and Full pricing on the CINEMA 70s S2 and 50 S2** (#18), and which tier gates custom target curves (#20).
- **The X3800H's Audyssey tier** (#21). XT rather than XT32 would move it below the CINEMA 50 models.
- **What Bass Control gains with one sub.** No source quantifies it, so the line between Best and Good is a capability judgement rather than a measured result.
- **Whether the MRX 540 gets STR-style automatic sub phase alignment.** If it does, ARC competes with Dirac Room Correction alone for a one-sub room.
- No two of these systems have been measured in the same room, so their order within a tier rests on features and price.
