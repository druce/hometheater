# Anthem MRX 540 8K

**The MRX 540 8K amplifies exactly the five channels this room uses and includes ARC Genesis at no charge, but the 540 itself has never been measured and ARC has no independent test.**

Ranked in: [Room correction & bass management](../criteria/room-correction.md) · [Music & two-channel performance](../criteria/music-performance.md) · [Streaming & multiroom](../criteria/streaming-and-multiroom.md) · [Amplification & pre-outs](../criteria/amplification-and-preouts.md) · [Connectivity](../criteria/connectivity.md) · [Usability, app & firmware support](../criteria/usability-and-support.md) · [Value, reliability & resale](../criteria/value-and-reliability.md).

## Verdict

Provisional. For [this room](../start-here/my-system.md) the 540 is a 5.1 receiver with nothing to pay after purchase: room correction with target-curve and crossover control is included, and Spotify Connect, AirPlay and Cast cover the streaming sources. Its main problem is evidence. The 540 is unmeasured, its larger siblings measure well but share no confirmed amplifier design with it, and ARC Genesis has never been tested against Dirac or Audyssey in the same room. At $1,899.99 it also costs more than the [Onkyo TX-RZ50](onkyo-tx-rz50.md), which includes Dirac.

The 1% THD basis is looser than Yamaha's 0.06% or Onkyo's 0.08%, so the 100 W is not directly comparable with their ratings; see [amplifier power ratings](../concepts/amplifier-power-ratings.md). With five amplifier channels all used in 5.1, there is nothing spare to bi-amp the fronts or power a second zone.

## Room correction

[ARC Genesis](../systems/arc-genesis.md) is a free Windows or Mac download and the calibrated microphone ships in the box (ARC Genesis page; MRX spec sheet, 2022-07-15). No paid tier and no Dirac option exist for the MRX line in any source read. Professional Mode exposes the target curve and per-speaker-set crossovers and slopes, and correction can extend to 20 kHz. Two limits matter here: ARC does not set speaker distances, which are entered by hand, and Anthem documents automatic speaker-to-sub phase alignment for its STR products only, so how well the 540 aligns one sub across the crossover is not established. The paralleled sub outputs cost nothing with one sub. See [bass management and crossovers](../concepts/bass-management-and-crossovers.md).

## Streaming

Anthem's current page lists AirPlay, Google Chromecast, Spotify Connect and Bluetooth, and file playback of FLAC, ALAC, WAV and DSD (anthemav.com, 2026-11-08). The 2022 spec sheet called it AirPlay 2 and promised Roon "coming soon"; Roon appears neither on the current page nor in the 2024 operating manual, and Roon's own partner list was not checked. No TIDAL Connect, Qobuz Connect, native Apple Music or internet radio is listed. See [streaming protocols](../systems/streaming-protocols.md).

- **Radio Paradise FLAC:** no native path, since the 540 lists no internet-radio service. The external streamer on coax or optical is the plan of record; Anthem states no input rate limit (open question #12). See [Radio Paradise](../systems/radio-paradise.md) and the [external-streamer comparison](../comparisons/radio-paradise-flac-native-vs-external-streamer.md).
- **Spotify:** Spotify Connect.
- **Apple Music:** AirPlay from an Apple device.

## Measurements

No independent measurement of the MRX 540 8K is in the evidence. Its siblings have been measured, but the 540 has a different amplifier count and its transformer and pre-amp board are unconfirmed, so these are proxies only:

- (proxy: MRX 740) Audioholics measured 145 W one channel and about 130 W two channels at 8 Ω, 20 Hz–20 kHz, under 0.1% THD, against a 140 W rating; 89 W/ch with five channels at 1 kHz, 1% THD; about 86 dB amplifier SINAD at 30 W, five channels; and 105 dB SINAD and 110 dB SNR from the pre-outs (Audioholics, 2022-03-17).
- (proxy: MRX 1140) Audioholics measured 153 W two channels at 8 Ω and current limiting to 163 W at 4 Ω. ASR rated its DAC very good to excellent and its amplifier average for the class, found it never shut down under test, and recommended it; ASR's table lists it at SINAD 81 with conditions unstated (ASR, 2025-11-04).
- (proxy: MRX 1140) With the subwoofer output disabled, DAC SINAD fell about 10 dB, which an Anthem representative had predicted to the reviewer. Whether the 540 behaves the same is unknown; with a sub enabled, as here, the favourable case applies if it does.

Reading these numbers: [measurements and audibility](../concepts/measurements-and-audibility.md).

## Known issues

No owner reports or firmware notes specific to the 540 are in the evidence, so its firmware state is unknown; the 2022 sheet promises over-the-air updates.

- (proxy: MRX 740/1140) Audioholics measured the bass-managed sub low-pass at 12 dB/octave on stock firmware; Anthem supplied beta firmware with 24 dB/octave, and whether a release carries it is unconfirmed.
- (proxy: MRX 740/1140) There is no analog bypass, so analog inputs are always digitised; a streamer on a digital input avoids this.
- (proxy: MRX 1140) The internal amplifier cannot be disabled, and the reconstruction filter attenuates poorly above 24 kHz, which ASR considered a non-problem at higher sample rates.
- Audioholics judged the 740/1140 amplifiers best with speakers of 6 Ω nominal or higher, consistent with the 540's above-4-Ω rating.

## Price and availability

$1,899.99, in stock on anthemav.com (2026-11-08), the manufacturer's own store; no independent retailer price was captured, and the launch MSRP is unknown. The MRX 740 8K sells for $3,099.99 on the same site. The spec sheet is dated 2022-07-15, which is the sheet's date rather than a confirmed release date. No successor and no warranty term appear in the evidence; the product page links a warranty section that was not captured.
