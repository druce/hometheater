# Which Dirac license to buy

**For one sub in a small room, buy Room Correction plus Bass Control wherever Bass Control is sold: $299 on the Onkyo TX-RZ50 and Arcam AVA15, and the $599 Full-bandwidth bundle on the Denon X3800H/X3900H and Marantz CINEMA 50 models.**

## Question

Three choices face a buyer of a [Dirac Live](../systems/dirac-live.md) receiver for a [5.1 room with one subwoofer](../start-here/my-system.md): Limited Room Correction (20–500 Hz, $259 on the X3900H) or Full (20–20,000 Hz, $349); whether Bass Control ($299) earns its price with a single sub; and how both answers change by receiver, since some include Room Correction and two cannot take Bass Control at all. Prices are dirac.com, 2026-11-08, from the reconciled table on the Dirac Live page. The Yamaha RX-A4A and Anthem MRX 540 8K carry no Dirac and are outside this question.

## Answer

**Limited versus Full is close to a non-decision once Bass Control is in the basket.** On the X3900H, Limited plus Bass Control bought separately is $558 and the Full bundle is $599. Bought alone, Limited at $259 saves $90 over Full, and upgrading later costs $99, so deferring costs $9 (Dirac X3900H page). The case for Limited rests on where a one-sub small room has its problems: [room modes](../concepts/room-modes-and-correction-limits.md) dominate the bass, and Limited's 20–500 Hz band covers that region. Full adds correction above 500 Hz, where the claimed gains (imaging, a larger sweet spot) are Dirac's own and no source in hand measures them. That makes Limited a defensible purchase only on a receiver where Bass Control is not offered and a $259 tier is actually sold, which Dirac shows only for the X3900H; the CINEMA 70s Series 2 and X2900H pages give a $259–$349 range without itemising tiers (open question #18).

**Bass Control is worth buying with one sub, on the strength of what it automates rather than on any measured gain.** Dirac says it time-aligns and levels a single sub against the speakers (Dirac Bass Control page, 2026-11-08), and the Arcam manual states the practical difference: with Room Correction alone the crossovers, sub distance and sub level are set by hand on the receiver, while Bass Control computes them (Arcam AVA15/AVA25 manual p.40). The marginal price is $250 on the Denon and Marantz 50 models ($599 bundle against $349 for Full alone) and $299 on the RZ50 and AVA15. Buying Full now and Bass Control later costs $648 on the X3900H, $49 more than the bundle, if the bundle prices hold; Dirac shows them against struck-through list prices that may mark a temporary discount. **No source quantifies the single-sub improvement**, and the one independent Dirac measurement in hand (Winer, PC Dirac, no sub, 2020-09) did not test Bass Control. Sub placement changes the starting point for every correction, so it comes before either licence.

**Two candidates stop at Room Correction.** On the Denon AVR-X2900H and Marantz CINEMA 70s Series 2, Dirac's grid marks Bass Control and ART not supported, so the single sub is integrated by hand or by the included Audyssey MultEQ XT. That is the main room-correction reason to step up to an X3900H, X3800H or RZ50.

**What would change this verdict.** An independent measurement of one sub with Bass Control against Room Correction with a hand-set crossover, distance and level would replace the argument from automation with a number; a small difference would make Bass Control optional on the Denon and Marantz models. Itemised tier prices on the CINEMA 70s S2 and 50 S2 (#18), the bandwidth of the Arcam's included tier and the NAD V3i's licence (#17), and an end to the bundle discount would each change a row below. Confirmation that one $299 Bass Control licence covers a second sub would strengthen it, since a second sub is the measured fix for seat-to-seat variation.

Whether a Dirac microphone comes with a Denon or Marantz licence is unknown; Onkyo, NAD and Arcam supply one. Onkyo's firmware log shows the RZ50's Bass Control crossover was applied incorrectly from the 2024-10-28 firmware until a 2025-07-01 fix that required re-exporting filters, so an RZ50 owner should run current firmware before calibrating (Onkyo firmware notes, 2026-03). ART ($299, and requiring Bass Control with a sub) is left out: its effect is a manufacturer claim with no independent measurement.
