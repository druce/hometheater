## Criteria (the spine)

- [Criterion 1: Room correction & bass management](criteria/room-correction.md): Room correction is the priority that most separates the candidates, and for one sub in a small room the included-vs-paid tier matters as much as the brand.
- [Criterion 2: Music & two-channel performance](criteria/music-performance.md): Measured two-channel performance separates careless designs from good ones, but above a threshold the room and the correction matter more than the next few dB of SINAD.
- [Criterion 3: Streaming & multiroom](criteria/streaming-and-multiroom.md): With an external streamer allowed, a receiver's own platform matters mainly for app quality and multiroom fit, and the Radio Paradise requirement moves to the streamer.
- [Criterion 4: Amplification & pre-outs](criteria/amplification-and-preouts.md): For 5.1 in a small room nearly every candidate has enough clean power, so protection behaviour and pre-outs for a later external amp are what differentiate.
- [Criterion 5: Connectivity](criteria/connectivity.md): Connectivity is close to a tie among current candidates, with the exceptions being 4K-only HDMI on the NAD and the digital input needed for an external streamer.
- [Criterion 6: Usability, app & firmware support](criteria/usability-and-support.md): Setup friction, app quality and the manufacturer's firmware record decide whether the room correction actually gets run and stays working.
- [Criterion 7: Value, reliability & resale](criteria/value-and-reliability.md): Value turns on what each step up buys for this room: the X3900H plus a Dirac license sets the reference price, against which both cheaper and premium options have to argue.

## Concepts

- [Bass management and crossovers](concepts/bass-management-and-crossovers.md): In a small room with one sub, the crossover setting and sub/main alignment decide bass quality more than the receiver's brand of room correction does.
- [Room modes and the limits of correction](concepts/room-modes-and-correction-limits.md): Room correction can pull down bass peaks at the main seat but cannot fill deep nulls, and its benefit shrinks within a foot or two of the measurement position.
- [Amplifier power ratings](concepts/amplifier-power-ratings.md): A receiver's rated watts say little until the load, the number of channels driven and the distortion threshold are stated, and protection modes can matter more than peak power.
- [Measurements and audibility](concepts/measurements-and-audibility.md): SINAD and pre-out figures separate well-engineered receivers from careless ones, but above roughly the high-80s dB most differences are below audibility and room correction dominates.
- [Lossless streaming paths](concepts/lossless-streaming-paths.md): Whether a stream arrives lossless depends on the path: a native service or URL keeps FLAC, AirPlay 2 carries ALAC at 44.1/16, and internet-radio directories often transcode.

## Systems

- [Audyssey MultEQ (XT, XT32, MultEQ-X)](systems/audyssey-multeq.md): XT32 is a capable included correction on Denon and Marantz's upper models, and MultEQ-X adds control for a one-time fee, but its support for the 2026 models is undocumented.
- [Dirac Live (Room Correction, Bass Control, ART)](systems/dirac-live.md): Dirac Live is the most widely available third-party correction, but what an owner actually gets depends on the license tier bought, and Bass Control and ART are not offered on every Dirac-capable receiver.
- [Anthem ARC Genesis](systems/arc-genesis.md): ARC Genesis gives detailed per-group control of correction and bass management on Anthem receivers, though its multi-sub handling and automatic phase alignment on the MRX are not documented as matching Dirac Bass Control.
- [Yamaha YPAO](systems/ypao.md): YPAO is included and has no upgrade path; on the RX-A4A it adds multipoint and 64-bit EQ, but there is no independent comparison placing it against Dirac or XT32.
- [HEOS (Denon, Marantz)](systems/heos.md): HEOS covers Spotify, TIDAL, Qobuz, AirPlay 2 and Roon Ready, but its internet radio is lossy-only and Apple Music arrives only via AirPlay, so Radio Paradise FLAC needs an external streamer.
- [Yamaha MusicCast](systems/musiccast.md): MusicCast is a broad multiroom system, but it has no documented lossless Radio Paradise path and down-converts linked rooms to 48 kHz.
- [BluOS (Bluesound, NAD)](systems/bluos.md): BluOS is the one platform with native Radio Paradise lossless and, since 2026-10-20, native Apple Music, which makes a NODE or NODE NANO the reference external streamer.
- [WiiM (WiiM Home, RoomFit)](systems/wiim.md): WiiM streamers are the low-cost alternative to a Bluesound NODE for feeding a receiver, but the Ultra and Amp Ultra lack AirPlay 2 and native Apple Music, and WiiM documents Radio Paradise as integrated only in its PC app.
- [Streaming protocols (AirPlay 2, Google Cast, Connect, Roon Ready)](systems/streaming-protocols.md): Spotify Connect is universal among the candidates, but AirPlay 2 is not: WiiM's Amp Ultra and Ultra lack it, which removes the Apple Music path on those devices.
- [Radio Paradise](systems/radio-paradise.md): Radio Paradise's lossless FLAC is native only on BluOS (and Sonos), with WiiM reported but unconfirmed, so for every other receiver the requirement is met by the external streamer.

## Products

- [Denon AVR-X3900H](products/denon-avr-x3900h.md): The current measurement leader among the candidates (ASR SINAD 102, recommended), with XT32 included and the full Dirac suite available as a paid add-on.
- [Denon AVR-X3800H](products/denon-avr-x3800h.md): The discounted previous generation, with the same Dirac eligibility as the X3900H but a measurably weaker DAC (ASR SINAD 87).
- [Denon AVR-X2900H](products/denon-avr-x2900h.md): The cheapest new Denon worth considering, held back by MultEQ XT (not XT32) and Dirac Room Correction only, with no Bass Control.
- [Marantz CINEMA 70s Series 2](products/marantz-cinema-70s-series-2.md): A slim music-oriented 7.2 receiver whose 50 W amps and MultEQ XT limit it, and whose Series 1 predecessor showed a distortion fault at 4 ohms.
- [Marantz CINEMA 50 Series 2](products/marantz-cinema-50-series-2.md): XT32 and the full Dirac suite at $3,000 buy channels and finish that a 5.1 room does not use, so it has to win on sound or styling to beat the X3900H.
- [Marantz CINEMA 50](products/marantz-cinema-50.md): The Series 1 CINEMA 50, discounted, keeps full Dirac eligibility but is measured only by a member review.
- [Yamaha RX-A4A](products/yamaha-rx-a4a.md): Still current and capable on power, but YPAO with no Dirac path and no native lossless Radio Paradise put it behind on both priority criteria.
- [Onkyo TX-RZ50](products/onkyo-tx-rz50.md): The only candidate with full-bandwidth Dirac included, undercut by ASR-measured power limiting on this platform that persists on its Pioneer sibling's February 2026 firmware.
- [Anthem MRX 540 8K](products/anthem-mrx-540-8k.md): An exact fit for 5.1 with two sub outputs and ARC Genesis, but unmeasured itself, with the larger MRX models as the only proxies.
- [NAD T 758 V3i](products/nad-t-758-v3i.md): The only receiver with BluOS built in, which no longer matters much now that an external streamer is acceptable; its V3 predecessor measured worst of all candidates.
- [Arcam AVA15](products/arcam-ava15.md): Full Dirac included with Bass Control and ART available, at $4,499.95 and with no measurements yet, so it has to justify a premium of more than double the X3900H.
- [WiiM Amp Ultra](products/wiim-amp-ultra.md): A well-measured stereo streaming amp (ASR recommended) for the future stereo rooms, whose lack of AirPlay 2 and native Apple Music conflicts with an owner requirement.
